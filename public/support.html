Founder Background

Who I Am

I'm Viktor Kryvotsiuk, born in Mykolaiv, Ukraine in 1985 and now based in Hoi An, Vietnam. For more than twenty years I've lived close to wind and water: kitesurfing, surfing, hydrofoiling, sailing, and paragliding. For more than ten of those years I've worked as a kitesurfing instructor in Vietnam, spending thousands of hours reading wind, current, weather, and risk in real conditions.

I'm also a self-taught engineer. I design hardware housings, 3D-print prototypes, write the firmware, configure tracking servers, and test everything in real saltwater rather than on a bench. I'm the father of a daughter named Vivi — she's a big part of why I keep building.

Earlier SmartSurf Work

Senlay grew out of my earlier SmartSurf work: a waterproof GPS tracker and safety layer for water-sport athletes.

That work forced one question again and again: what is really happening at this exact coordinate, right now? To answer it, I had to fuse weather stations, ocean buoys, tide gauges, swell models, air-quality networks, and satellite data into one usable picture.

Why Senlay Exists

After building that fusion layer, I realised AI agents have the same missing context. They can reason about wind, waves, terrain, and risk, but without live measured data they still guess.

Senlay turns that physical-world context into an API any AI agent — or any developer — can call. Same hardware-first principle: when a real METAR cup spinning on a runway disagrees with a forecast model, trust the cup. Senlay cross-references both and flags the discrepancy.

Common Questions

How do I get an API key?

Register at senlay.world/register — free, no credit card required. You'll get 100 requests/day immediately. AI agents can also use /api/v1/agent-register with no password.

What counts as one request?

Each call to /api/v1/pwm or /api/v1/sense counts as one request. Cached responses (same coordinate within 5 minutes) don't count toward your limit.

Which locations are supported?

Any coordinate on Earth. Sensor density varies — coastal and urban areas have the most hardware sensors. Inland and remote areas rely more on model data (still accurate, just less cross-referenced).

How fresh is the data?

Model data updates every 15 minutes. Hardware sensors (METAR, NDBC buoys, NOAA tide gauges) report every 5–60 minutes depending on the station. Senlay caches each coordinate for 5 minutes.

Can I use Senlay with my own AI model?

Yes. The /api/v1/pwm endpoint returns a context_string you can inject into any LLM — Claude, GPT-4, Llama, Mistral, anything. See the docs.

I'm building an AI agent — how do I integrate?

Use /api/v1/agent-register to get a key without passwords, then call /api/v1/sense for plain-text output optimized for LLM consumption. See agentic tooling docs.

What if a sensor is wrong?

Hardware sensors win over models. Senlay cross-references both and flags discrepancies in the PWM context. If you spot bad data, email me with the coordinates and timestamp — I'll investigate.
