SmartSurf — Where This All Started

My main project is SmartSurf — an AI-powered safety and coaching platform for water-sport athletes, built around a dedicated waterproof GPS tracker.

The tracker is a sealed, button-less device cast in epoxy resin, IP68 to 10m, charged by solar and wireless, controlled magnetically, with global-roaming SIM and an SOS sequence built in. It mounts to your board, knows when a session starts, detects when board and rider drift apart, and can fire an alert when something goes wrong. The companion app (app.smartsurf.global) overlays your GPS track with real environmental conditions at your exact time and location and turns it into discipline-specific coaching for twin-tip, kitefoil, wing foil, windsurf, and SUP.

SmartSurf wasn't invented for a pitch deck. It came directly from years of watching boards drift away, riders get lost, wind drop without warning, and instructors lose visual contact with students at the worst possible moment. It's been in development since the COVID period, through prototype after prototype, and is now in real use with beta riders. Self-funded. Zero investors. Built one part at a time.

Why Senlay Exists

To do its job, SmartSurf needs the truth about the water and the sky — right now, at the rider's exact coordinates. That meant fusing METAR stations, ocean buoys, NOAA tide gauges, swell models, air-quality networks, and satellite data into one coherent picture. After years of building that fusion layer for SmartSurf, I realised something: AI agents are blind in exactly the same way SmartSurf riders used to be. They can write essays about wind shear and swell period, but they've never sensed a single gust. They guess. They hallucinate. They confidently invent conditions.

Senlay is the sensor-fusion backbone from SmartSurf, opened up so any AI agent — or any developer — can call it. Same hardware-first principle: when a real METAR cup spinning on a runway disagrees with a forecast model, trust the cup. Senlay cross-references both and flags the discrepancy.

Common Questions

How do I get an API key?

Register at senlay.world/register — free, no credit card required. You'll get 100 requests/day immediately. AI agents can also use /api/v1/agent-register with no password.

What counts as one request?

Each call to /api/v1/pwm or /api/v1/sense counts as one request. Cached responses (same coordinate within 5 minutes) don't count toward your limit.

Which locations are supported?

Any coordinate on Earth. Sensor density varies — coastal and urban areas have the most hardware sensors. Inland and remote areas rely more on model data (still accurate, just less cross-referenced).

How fresh is the data?

Model data updates every 15 minutes. Hardware sensors (METAR, NDBC buoys, NOAA tide gauges) report every 5–60 minutes depending on the station. Senlay caches each coordinate for 5 minutes.

Can I use Senlay with my own AI model?

Yes. The /api/v1/pwm endpoint returns a context_string you can inject into any LLM — Claude, GPT-4, Llama, Mistral, anything. See the docs.

I'm building an AI agent — how do I integrate?

Use /api/v1/agent-register to get a key without passwords, then call /api/v1/sense for plain-text output optimized for LLM consumption. See agentic tooling docs.

What if a sensor is wrong?

Hardware sensors win over models. Senlay cross-references both and flags discrepancies in the PWM context. If you spot bad data, email me with the coordinates and timestamp — I'll investigate.

Is there a paid tier?

Pro tier ($29/month, 10,000 req/day) is on the waitlist — join from the dashboard. Enterprise is custom — email me.
